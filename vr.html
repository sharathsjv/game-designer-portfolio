Interaction systems

Build it once, reuse it everywhere

Most modules needed the same core interactions: grabbing, touching, pressing UI and getting feedback. I built these as reusable systems in Unity so I didn't rebuild the same mechanics for every project, and new modules could start from working parts.

I also made environment templates, such as a fire safety module, that later projects could reuse.

In VR the feel of an interaction is most of the experience, so I spent much of my time making grabs and touches responsive, readable and not clunky.

Evaluation

Assessments that teach

I reworked how trainees are evaluated inside the simulations. The aim was to make it feel less like ticking boxes and more like learning: player actions, feedback and outcomes all tie back to what the module is meant to teach.

In the procedure module above, the trainee has to sequence the steps before doing them, so the order of the task is practised, not just read.

Experience centre

Showing a product, not just training on it

Not every module was training. For an automotive client I worked on a VR experience centre: you start from a lobby, step into a car interior and explore its cabin, controls and display up close.

Pace

From rough idea to working module in days

A lot of the work was fast-paced. I got used to taking a module from a rough idea to a solid, working VR build in a couple of days, including intensive modules delivered in a two-day bootcamp turnaround, while working closely with engineers, artists and other designers.
